# Design

The interface is a party game on a table: a yellow table, white stickers with a thick ink outline and a hard shadow, big round corners and bold type. Whatever can be pressed is pushed into its shadow when pressed; a revealed card stays pressed. Tokens: `client/src/styles/tokens.css`; shared styles: `base.css`; fonts: `fonts.ts`.

## Tokens

Every color, font, outline, corner, shadow and page width is a variable in `tokens.css`, and components take them from there, never as a literal value; the numbers that only lay a component out, such as a card's type sizes and the offset of a badge, are its own. Media queries can't read variables; the breakpoints are 40, 48, 60 and 64 rem. A second look, such as a dark one, is another block redefining the same variables under an attribute on `<html>`, nothing else.

## Fonts

Four web fonts from npm, served by our own server (`docs/rules/constraints.md`): ZCOOL KuaiLe for Chinese headings and status sentences, Baloo 2 for numbers and the Latin of headings, Noto Sans SC for text, and Nanum Pen Script, a pencil's hand, for the points that rise in a game alone, which are digits and signs: only its Latin slice is declared, about 15 KB, which a browser downloads with the first of them, showing Baloo 2 until it is there (`font-display: swap`). All four are under the SIL Open Font License. `--font-display` names Baloo 2 before ZCOOL KuaiLe, so a heading takes Latin from the one and Chinese from the other, with `font-synthesis: none`, since ZCOOL KuaiLe has one weight. The Chinese fonts come sliced by character range (93 and 101 `@font-face` rules), so a browser downloads only the slices it needs; the build keeps every slice as a file (`assetsInlineLimit: 0`), because a `data:` URL breaks the Content Security Policy.

## Rules

- **Contrast.** Text reads at 4.5:1 or more: small text on red is ink (white would be 3.4:1), on blue white, on yellow and the tints ink; red and blue text on white or yellow use the deep shades; hints use `--ink-2`. Nothing dims small text: a card's color label at 80% would fall to 3.9:1 on red and on blue. Default avatars are bright colors with an ink initial (tested). A card that shows a color carries its label too.
- **Type.** Headings and the status sentence in the display fonts; numbers in `.num`; the rest in Noto Sans SC at 500, 700 for labels, 900 for buttons and cards. No small capitals, italics, section numbers, hairlines or dotted leaders.
- **Stickers.** Panels, cards, buttons and inputs have an ink outline and a hard shadow. Whatever can be pressed, because it does something or opens something, has the hard shadow and moves the same way (`press` in `base.css`): on a computer, a hover lifts it a pixel with its shadow a pixel longer; pressing moves it by its shift and drops the shadow; the small stickers, a title's pill, a tag, a badge or the button of a replay, take the small shadow and shift (`press-small`); a long press is the cards' alone. A choice is not a button: a checkbox, a radio, a row or a pill to pick one of, and the two tabs of the sign-in form, have an outline and no shadow, and the chosen one turns yellow. A button's color says what it does (`UiButton.vue`): a main action is green (`--go`), with the outline and the shadow of every button, the others are white, one that can't be undone white with red type, and a disabled one grey and flat. Ink is for what can't be pressed, a tag or a badge filled with ink, and for a toast, which a tap dismisses and which moves when pressed without a shadow (`press-flat`); nothing else that can't be pressed, a bubble or a cheer, has a shadow. Avatars are outlined circles. On the friends list, whether a friend is online is a small outlined dot on the bottom right corner, green or grey; in a room, someone the room waits for, offline or invited and not yet there, has a faded avatar, under a white veil, with three grey dots in the middle that light up in turn, as a chat shows someone typing, or stay still where the device asks for less motion; nothing marks those who are there. An avatar with the hard shadow of a sticker that can be pressed opens something, and every avatar that opens something has it: a player's panel in a room or a replay, their page elsewhere, the account page from the masthead, the sheet to invite friends from their stack, the practice room from Reviewer 1's button, and the GitHub page of an issue's author among the contributions, whose avatar is GitHub's mark, white on an ink disc in the same outlined circle (`GithubAvatar.vue`), bundled with the site and used as GitHub allows, as a button to a page on GitHub; the others' choices on a card's corner, a tap on which chooses the card, have none. Tap targets are at least 44 px; where avatars overlap, as the students in a group's bar, each one's is the part of it that shows.
- **Layout.** One column on phones with 16 px margins, never scrolling sideways. From 60 rem the home page has two columns; from 64 rem the game three (the group chat, open all the time, and the groups; the board; the clue log and the tools, among them a match's progress, which opens in a sheet) and the waiting room two, each column stacked from the top. On a phone the board comes first, the action bar follows it, and the groups, the clue log, the chat and a match's progress open as sheets from the bar. A game's clock sticks to the top of the screen, with the chair's button to pause after it and the reaction button at the end (on their own in a game without a clock, and the reaction button alone once the game is over), and in the waiting room the room's masthead does, with the reaction button left of the room menu, and the start bar under it, all under the reconnecting banner while that shows: the chair's with the line on whether the room is ready and, under it, the shuffle, the invitation of friends and the start button sharing their row evenly; a member's with the line and the invitation on one row. Cards grow with the board, on a big screen with its height.
- **Points that rise** in a game ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#points-as-they-come)): handwritten in `--font-hand`, in the group's deep shade, with no ground but a ring of paper drawn with shadows around each stroke, so that they read on a card of their own color; they rise a short way and fade, or only fade under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and let every tap through. On a card about a third of its height; big in the middle of the screen at the end.
- **Cards.** Hidden: white. The viewer's choice: yellow. Known but hidden: the tint, labelled in the corner. Revealed: the full color, pressed in; irrelevant papers struck through; Reviewer 2 ink with yellow type and a slanted stamp. A card revealed in front of the viewer turns over: its face turns away as it lay, white, the viewer's yellow or, for a PI or on a marked card, the tint it showed (Reviewer 2's ink for a PI), its color comes on the way back, and it lands pressed into the table; on Reviewer 2 the stamp slams down once the card has landed. Only a card revealed live turns: a board that catches up after a reload, a locked screen or a pause shows its cards as they lie, and a replay turns a card only on the step that reveals it. Where the device asks for less motion, the card is revealed at once. The others' choices hang as avatars on a card's corner; the card's accessible name says who they are. A group's marks, for its students and its PI alike: a round sticker hanging on the bottom right corner with a tick, a cross or an exclamation mark, which the card's accessible name says in words; for the students the card takes the tint of the color the mark stands for, the group's own, the other group's or `--r2-tint`, unless it is the viewer's choice, and for the PI, who only reads the marks, it keeps the tint of its own color. A student sets them from a menu that a long press on the card opens, or a right click (`MarkMenu.vue`), with no tools to pick, and a line under the board says what a tap and a press do; a long press selects no text and opens no menu of the browser's.
- **Original.** The look is ours, as [rules/constraints.md](rules/constraints.md) asks: the yellow table, the stickers with their outline and hard shadow, the display fonts and the stamped rejection are what sets it apart from the board game that inspired the first game. Keep it that way when adding to it, and don't borrow that game's art, icons or card designs.
- **Sheets** rise from the bottom on phones and are dialogs on wider screens, closed by a button or Escape; one opened from another lies over it and alone takes the keys, and they lie in the order they were opened, whatever their order on the page, under the quick reactions, the toasts and the menu of marks. A sheet's head, its title between its round buttons, stays at its top while the content scrolls under it, with a soft shadow under it once the content has scrolled, as under a page's masthead. A sheet with a way back hides the sheet it leads back to, which comes back as it was: on phones it slides up from the bottom, and down again as the viewer goes back, and as a dialog it rises a little into place, while the sheet under it fades out and in. A sheet comes with its scrim fading in as the panel rises; closed, the panel sinks a little and fades out while the scrim fades, quicker than it came (`--motion-quick`), and nothing in it can be pressed meanwhile. Where the device asks for less motion, nothing slides or fades.
- **Links.** A link in text is the link blue (`--link`), with no underline until a pointer is over it, when an underline sweeps in from the left, under every line of a link that wraps, and the link deepens; pressed, the underline is there at once, and it eases away after (`text-link` in `base.css`). On a tinted ground, the yellow table or the beige row of a list, where the link blue would fall under 4.5:1, a link takes `--blue-deep`, which reads there (`on-tint`). A link that leads off the site ends in an arrow (`LinkArrow.vue`) that moves out under a pointer and further under a press: the link on a player's page; among the contributions, the GitHub username of an issue's author, in the deep blue of the beige row, after GitHub's mark, and the way to GitHub's contributors in the card's intro. The issue numbers in the list of contributions stay grey, on purpose.
- **Titles.** A title that a tap explains (`TitleInfo.vue`) looks as its place shows it, with no underline anywhere: a pill, a badge or a tag is a small sticker with the small shadow and the press; a title in a heading or in a line of text darkens under a pointer or a press, to the deeper of the deep shade it stands in (`--red-deeper`, `--blue-deeper`), which moves it down a pixel. A decision takes the deep shade of its group, in the letter and on the record; a title that stands in text takes the color of its news (`titleTone` in `titles.ts`), blue for good and red for bad, in bold. The big headings keep their colors: a streak card's, a match's ranking's.
- **Motion.** Motion moves and fades, and lays nothing out anew, apart from a fold, whose height eases open and shut over 220 ms, its content fading in, while its marker turns to point down; it eases with `--ease-out` as it comes, `--ease-in` as it leaves and `--ease-spring` as it pops, a mark on a card or a heart just liked. A short message slides down a little as it comes and up as it goes, fading either way, and the messages left move to their places; as a page comes, what is under its masthead fades in, rising a little, while the masthead stands still and nothing waits for the page before. Where the device asks for less motion, everything arrives at once.
- **What stays at the top.** A page keeps one bar at the top of the screen as it scrolls: a bar of its own, if it has one to keep there, while the masthead scrolls away, and the masthead otherwise. A game keeps its clock there; the waiting room keeps its masthead, which holds the room's number and menu, with the start bar under it. The account page, a player's page and the pages that mails lead to have their title on a bar under the masthead, as tall as it, with the way back to the page before at its start when the page was reached from another on the site; that bar then stays at the top, with the masthead's shadow under it. A page opened on its own, as from a shared link or a mail, has no way back, and neither does one whose page before is used up, as the account page after a password is reset with a mail's link; there the masthead stays. The wordmark is in the same place at the top of every page.
- **Writing.** All interface text lives in the locale files: `@intlify/vue-i18n/no-raw-text` fails on text in a template, and a test checks both languages have the same keys.
