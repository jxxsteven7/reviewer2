# Consistent display

Every page and every game is one site, and looks and works as one: a player who knows one game finds everything in the next where they expect it. This file is the one place for the rules of what players see. Read it before changing anything players see, and follow it. A PR that adds a new kind of element, or changes how one looks or works, changes this file in the same PR, so that every place shows it the same way. The values themselves, the tokens, fonts and icons, are listed in [../design.md](../design.md).

## One look

- **One table for every game.** The interface is a party game on a table: a yellow table, white stickers with a thick ink outline and a hard shadow, big round corners and bold type. Every game takes it as it is, with no colors, fonts, outlines, shadows, corners or motion of its own. What sets a game apart is its board and what lies on it, built from the same parts.
- **Tokens only.** Every color, font, outline, corner, shadow and page width is a variable in `client/src/styles/tokens.css`, and so is the motion every page shares, the press's `--motion`, a sheet's and the three easings; components take them from there, never as a literal value. The numbers that only lay a component out, such as a card's type sizes and the offset of a badge, and the timing of a motion that is a component's own, as a card turning over or the waiting dots, are its own. Media queries can't read variables; the breakpoints are 40, 48, 60 and 64 rem, and a rule for phones alone is `max-width: 39.99rem`. A second look, such as a dark one, is another block redefining the same variables under an attribute on `<html>`, nothing else.
- **Shared parts, never copies.** Build from the components in `client/src/core/components` and the classes of `base.css`: `.page`, `.panel`, `.display`, `.section-title`, `.kicker`, `.sr-only`, the presses and `text-link`. Don't write a part's look again elsewhere. A part that a second game needs, such as the first game's clock bar, whose-turn strip, rules sheet, paused notice, chat, replay controls, rosters or decision letter, moves to `core`, and both games use it.
- **One thing, one look.** The same kind of thing looks and works the same on every page and in every game: a button for the same kind of action, an avatar that opens something, a link, a choice, a tag, a sheet, someone who is offline. A second look for the same thing is a bug.
- **Original.** The look is ours, as [constraints.md](constraints.md) asks: the yellow table, the stickers with their outline and hard shadow, the display fonts and the stamped rejection are what sets it apart from the board game that inspired the first game. Keep it that way when adding to it, and don't borrow that game's art, icons or card designs.

## A game's room

Every game's room is built the same way, so that a player of one finds their way in another.

- **The masthead** holds the room's number, the reaction button and the room menu, and no account entry. In the waiting room it stays at the top, with the start bar under it.
- **The clock bar** stays at the top during a game: the clock (`ClockBar.vue`), the chair's button to pause after it and the reaction button at the end; on their own in a game without a clock, and the reaction button alone once the game is over. The clock is red while the viewer has to act, and ink at any other time, for spectators and on a big screen too.
- **The whose-turn strip** above the board is white: a short line says who the viewer is, and a sentence in the display font says what happens now. In a game of groups it takes the viewer's group's tint while it is their group's turn, where its deep-shaded lines still read, at 4.9:1 on red and 5.5:1 on blue. In a game where everyone knows which players are AI, while an AI makes the move, the sentence says that it is thinking, for everyone, and a grey line under it, small and bold, says for how long it may (`WhoseTurn.vue`); a game that keeps the AI players hidden until the end says nothing of it.
- **On a phone** the board comes first and the action bar follows it: small buttons sharing the row evenly, their labels on one line, which open the rest as sheets, with the count of unread messages on the chat's corner. On a wide screen the rest stands in columns beside the board, each stacked from the top. The rarer tools, how to play, reviewing the game and ending it, are quiet small buttons.
- **How to play.** Every game has a rules sheet: the room's settings first, then the rules, then the way to the tutorial, which opens in a new tab from a room so that the game goes on.
- **A pause** shows a dashed panel that says so, with the chair's button to go on.
- **The end** shows the result as a letter: a panel with a tinted pill naming who it is for, the verdict in the display font, the text and a signature; then the players ranked in rows. The chair gets the main button for what comes next, and everyone else a grey line saying that the chair decides.
- **Rating the game.** Under the result of a game that finished, a panel asks how it was (`RatingCard.vue`), in every game alike: the question, with the round × in its head that dismisses it, what is rated, such as the pair of words, and the game's options as pills to pick, which turn yellow when chosen and may be changed while the room shows the game; then a grey hint and "Say something", a secondary button that opens the feedback with the game attached.
- **A match** has one table in every game (`MatchTable.vue`): which game it is, then a line for each player with the place, the avatar, the name, the total and the points of the game in focus, the viewer's line yellow, and a grey line under its head where the game that has just ended didn't count, saying why. It comes first in the waiting room. During a game it opens in a sheet, "Match progress", from the action bar on a phone and the tools on a wide screen, and the chair's quiet button to end the match stands with the rarer tools; once a game is over, the table follows the rating card, with where each player's points came from under their name. A game whose points come only at its end has no column of the game in focus while one is on, and in a game whose players are numbers the table shows nobody as offline or gone while one is on, and its lines keep their order whoever leaves. Once the match is over, ended early or not, its final ranking (`MatchResults.vue`) takes the table's place; in the waiting room it also takes the place of the start bar, the desk and the line saying that the room is waiting, so that nothing offers to start a game that can't start. A game whose matches award no titles yet says nothing of titles there.
- **Replays** step through a game with "step n of m", back, a slider and forward, and the step's lines on a tinted block.
- **Chat** shows messages as beige rows, the viewer's yellow, each with the avatar, the name in bold and the text. The field and a green send button share a row, with the count of characters at its end, and a grey line says when the viewer may only read.

## Pressing

- **Stickers.** Panels, cards and buttons have an ink outline and a hard shadow; fields have the outline and no shadow. Whatever can be pressed, because it does something or opens something, has the hard shadow and moves the same way (`press` in `base.css`): on a computer, a hover lifts it a pixel with its shadow a pixel longer; pressing moves it by its shift and drops the shadow. The small stickers, a title's pill, a tag, a badge or the button of a replay, take the small shadow and shift (`press-small`). A long press is the cards' alone. Two things that can be pressed have no shadow: a quiet button, and the title row of a fold, which only tint under a pointer.
- **Ink** is for what can't be pressed, a tag or a badge filled with ink, and for a toast, which a tap dismisses and which moves when pressed without a shadow (`press-flat`). Nothing else that can't be pressed, a bubble or a cheer, has a shadow.
- **Taps and focus.** Tap targets are at least 44 px; where a sticker must look smaller, an invisible box around it (`::before`) takes the tap. Where avatars overlap, as the students in a group's bar, each one's is the part of it that shows. Hover styles apply only where there is a pointer (`@media (hover: hover)`). Focus shows a 3 px ring in `--focus`, 2 px out, around a field's box, a choice's label or an avatar, and in ink on a group's color.

## Buttons

- **Always `UiButton`.** Its color says what it does: `primary`, a main action, is green (`--go`); `secondary`, the others, white; `danger`, an action that ends, leaves or deletes something, white with red type and outline; `quiet`, with no outline or shadow, is for Cancel, Ignore, Retry, a game's rarer tools and the foot of a page. A move in a game is a main action, even one that can't be taken back.
- **Sizes.** `large` for the one action a page is for, as the home page's start; the normal size in sheets and forms; `small` in bars, rows and mastheads.
- **States.** A disabled button is grey and flat, never faded. While an action is being sent, its button is disabled (`useBusy`), with no spinner and the same label. A button held back for a while, as a reaction too soon after another, looks disabled but stays tappable, and a tap says why in a toast.
- **Round buttons** in a sheet's head and a page's title bar are circles of 44 px with the outline, the shadow and the press: × to close, an arrow to go back (`UiSheet.vue`, `BackButton.vue`).
- **A button that stays on**, as a like, is white and turns green while it is on (`aria-pressed`).
- **Where they go.** In a sheet, one above the other at full width; in a form, the main button first, as wide as its label, with a quiet Cancel after it; in a bar, sharing the row evenly.
- **Asking first.** An action that can't be undone and ends or removes something asks first, in a sheet titled with the action: a question, the danger button repeating the title, and a quiet Cancel.

## Choices and fields

- **A choice is not a button.** A checkbox, a radio, a row or a pill to pick one of, the two tabs of the sign-in form and the tabs of a record, one for each game (`GameTabs.vue`), have an outline and no shadow, and the chosen one turns yellow; one that can't be chosen now is grey. Checkboxes are the browser's own, in ink, on a label row 44 px high, and there are no switches: turning the sounds on and off is a checkbox too. Radios are pills, or rows with the option in bold and a hint in grey (`PlayChoice.vue`, and `ChoiceRow.vue` in a form, for checkboxes too). A number picked from a few fixed values is a pill, as the 3, 5 or 7 games of a match of the second game; a number that may be anything in a range is typed (`inputmode="numeric"`), with no steppers.
- **Fields are `UiField`.** The label above in bold; the box with the strong outline and no shadow; the value at 16 px, so that phones don't zoom; the hint under it in grey. An error takes the hint's place in red and turns the outline red; a field that can't be changed is dashed on beige. A count of characters is the hint, as "n/max", and the error once it is over.
- **Errors.** A field's error shows under the field; a refusal of a whole form, as a red line under it (`.problem`, with `role="alert"`); anything else, in a toast. Every error the server sends has its text under `errors.<code>` in the locale files.
- **Warnings** of something that still works but may go worse, as times too short for Reviewer 1 to think, are a line in `--red-deep`, small and bold, beside what they are about: under the seats, and at the desk under the times as they are typed (`role="status"`). They block nothing and have no box, icon or button.

## Panels, rows and tags

- **Panels** are `.panel`: the strong outline, the panel's corners and shadow. A hero sticker, the one a page is about, takes the hero's corners and shadow. A small sticker that can't be pressed, as a notice or a room in a list, takes the thinner outline, the control's corners and the small shadow. A panel shown inside a sheet drops its own frame and title.
- **Rows** inside a panel are flat beige blocks, with no outline or shadow; the viewer's own row is yellow, and a group's takes its tint.
- **A dashed outline** means not yet or not now: a game to come, a pause, a field that can't be changed.
- **Tags.** An ink pill states a fact about someone, as "you", the chair or someone who left; an outlined white pill marks an AI player, or a record's kind; a pill in a group's tint names a group, as on a decision letter. Both are `.tag-pill` in `base.css`, the white one `outlined`. A count on a corner is an ink badge.
- **Beta.** A game still being tested has "Beta" beside its name on the home page's card: a yellow pill with the outline and the small shadow and press, which opens a sheet on what the beta means, that a big update may clear the game's records, and where to say something about it (`BetaPill.vue`). The same pill starts a line on a beige row where the game's rooms are set up, in the room setup and the practice sheet, and above its records (`BetaNote.vue`). Nowhere else marks it.
- **Folds** are stickers with the thinner outline and the small shadow, whose whole title row, at least 52 px high, is their button, with a ▸ marker that turns to point down as the fold opens.
- **Stats** are blocks with the thin outline, a small grey label over the value in the number font (`StatBlocks.vue`).
- **Empty and loading** are one grey sentence, with no spinner or skeleton; a whole page loading says so in ink. After a failure, Retry is a quiet small button.
- **Stamps** are slanted, outlined in the thin line, with the small corners, as Reviewer 2's rejection.

## Avatars

- **Avatars are outlined circles** (`PlayerAvatar.vue`): small in a line of text, medium in mastheads, rankings and menus, large on a panel. Default avatars are bright colors with an ink initial (tested at 4.5:1 or more), a guest's chosen by their number; an AI player's face is its icon (`AgentIcon.vue`).
- **An avatar with the hard shadow** of a sticker that can be pressed opens something, and every avatar that opens something has it (`AvatarButton.vue`; `avatarShadows.test.ts` checks it): a player's panel in a room or a replay, their page elsewhere, the account page from the masthead, the sheet to invite friends from their stack, the sheet to play with Reviewer 1 from its button, and the GitHub page of an issue's author among the contributions, whose avatar is GitHub's mark, white on an ink disc in the same outlined circle (`GithubAvatar.vue`), bundled with the site and used as GitHub allows, as a button to a page on GitHub. The others' choices on a card's corner, a tap on which chooses the card, have none.
- **Who is there.** On the friends list, a friend who is online has a small outlined green dot on the bottom right corner, and one who isn't has none. In a room, someone the room waits for, offline or invited and not yet there, has a faded avatar, under a white veil, with three grey dots in the middle that light up in turn, as a chat shows someone typing, or stay still where the device asks for less motion; an avatar too small for the dots has the veil alone. Nothing marks those who are there. The name of someone who is offline is grey (`--ink-2`), in a room and on the friends list alike (`PlayerName.vue`); nothing else about them fades, no pill and no other line of text.
- **Stacks.** Avatars in a stack overlap, and a "+n" after them counts the rest and opens nothing.
- **Reviewer 1.** In a waiting room, the chair's invitation of Reviewer 1 to a seat is a beige chip on the seat's row, with Reviewer 1's avatar as such a sticker, a plus on its corner, and the words (`InviteAgent.vue`); while the AI players are offline, it is held back, as a button is: grey words, a grey avatar with no shadow and a grey plus, and a tap says why in a toast; seated, Reviewer 1 has a player's avatar, which opens its page.

## Links

- **A link to a page** in text is the link blue (`--link`), with no underline until a pointer is over it, when an underline sweeps in from the left, under every line of a link that wraps, and the link deepens; pressed, the underline is there at once, and it eases away after (`text-link` in `base.css`). On a tinted ground, the yellow table or the beige row of a list, where the link blue would fall under 4.5:1, a link takes `--blue-deep`, which reads there (`on-tint`).
- **A word that opens a sheet** on the same page, as "Rules" on the home page, is ink and underlined, with a reach of 44 px; the tutorial's link beside it matches it. A link has no other look.
- **A link that leads off the site** ends in an arrow (`LinkArrow.vue`) that moves out under a pointer and further under a press: the link on a player's page; among the contributions, the GitHub username of an issue's author, in the deep blue of the beige row, after GitHub's mark, and the way to GitHub's contributors in the card's intro; and the version at the foot of the home page, which leads to the history up to it on GitHub, in grey, darkening to ink. The issue numbers in the list of contributions stay grey too, on purpose. Such a link has `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, and a player's own `rel="nofollow ugc noopener"`. A link out of a room opens in a new tab, so that the game goes on.
- **A link inside a sentence** is a slot of `<i18n-t>`; the locale files hold no HTML.

## Titles

- A title that a tap explains (`TitleInfo.vue`) looks as its place shows it, with no underline anywhere: a pill, a badge or a tag is a small sticker with the small shadow and the press; a title in a heading or in a line of text darkens under a pointer or a press, to the deeper of the deep shade it stands in (`--red-deeper`, `--blue-deeper`), which moves it down a pixel.
- A decision takes the deep shade of its group, in the letter and on the record; a title that stands in text takes the color of its news (`titleTone` in `titles.ts`), blue for good and red for bad, in bold, and so does a change of points in a ranking. The big headings keep their colors: a streak card's, a match's ranking's.

## Sheets, toasts and notices

- **Sheets** are `UiSheet`. They rise from the bottom on phones and are dialogs on wider screens, closed by a button, Escape or a tap beside them; one opened from another lies over it and alone takes the keys, and they lie in the order they were opened, whatever their order on the page, under the quick reactions, the toasts and the menu of marks. A sheet's head, its title between its round buttons, stays at its top while the content scrolls under it, with a soft shadow under it once the content has scrolled, as under a page's masthead. A sheet with a way back hides the sheet it leads back to, which comes back as it was: on phones it slides up from the bottom, and down again as the viewer goes back, and as a dialog it rises a little into place, while the sheet under it fades out and in. A sheet comes with its scrim fading in as the panel rises; closed, the panel sinks a little and fades out while the scrim fades, quicker than it came (`--motion-quick`), and nothing in it can be pressed meanwhile.
- **Toasts** are ink, at most three, each gone after a few seconds or at a tap, under the reconnecting banner (`UiToasts.vue`). A notice that asks for an answer, as an invitation or a request to join, lies among them as a small white sticker: the avatar, the line, a small main button and a quiet Ignore.

## Layout

- **Columns.** One column on phones, with 16 px margins, 24 px from 40 rem, never scrolling sideways. From 60 rem the home page has two columns. A form is at most 26 rem wide, a card to read 34 rem, and a room takes `--page-wide`. Cards grow with the board, on a big screen with its height.
- **What stays at the top.** A page keeps one bar at the top of the screen as it scrolls: a bar of its own, if it has one to keep there, while the masthead scrolls away, and the masthead otherwise. A game keeps its clock bar there; the waiting room keeps its masthead, which holds the room's number and menu, with the start bar under it: the chair's with the line on whether the room is ready and, under it, the shuffle, the invitation of friends and the start button sharing their row evenly; a member's with the line and the invitation on one row. The account page, a player's page and the pages that mails lead to have their title on a bar under the masthead, as tall as it, with the way back to the page before at its start when the page was reached from another on the site; that bar then stays at the top, with the masthead's shadow under it. A page opened on its own, as from a shared link or a mail, has no way back, and neither does one whose page before is used up, as the account page after a password is reset with a mail's link; there the masthead stays. Every bar that stays reaches the edges of the screen in the table's yellow and stays under the reconnecting banner while that shows. The wordmark is in the same place at the top of every page.

## Type, numbers and text

- **Contrast.** Text reads at 4.5:1 or more: small text on red is ink (white would be 3.4:1), on blue white, on yellow and the tints ink; red and blue text on white or yellow use the deep shades; hints use `--ink-2`. Nothing dims small text: a card's color label at 80% would fall to 3.9:1 on red and on blue. A card that shows a color carries its label too.
- **Type.** Headings and the status sentence in the display fonts; numbers in `.num`; the rest in Noto Sans SC at 500, 700 for labels, 900 for buttons and cards. A small label over what it names, as who the viewer is or a column's head, is in letter-spaced capitals at the smallest size. No small capitals, italics, section numbers, hairlines or dotted leaders.
- **Numbers.** A real minus sign (−), never a hyphen; a plus for points won; a dash (–) for none; ∞ for no limit (`points.ts`). A middle dot (·) joins the parts of a line, and an ellipsis is one character (…), never three dots.
- **Icons** are inline SVG, drawn in `currentcolor` with round ends and hidden from screen readers; there is no icon font. Emoji appear only in a room's calls (📢) and the heart of a like.
- **Writing.** All interface text lives in the locale files: `@intlify/vue-i18n/no-raw-text` fails on text in a template, and a test checks both languages have the same keys.

## Motion

- Motion moves and fades, and lays nothing out anew, apart from a fold, whose height eases open and shut over 220 ms, its content fading in, while its marker turns to point down. It eases with `--ease-out` as it comes, `--ease-in` as it leaves and `--ease-spring` as it pops, a mark on a card or a heart just liked.
- A short message slides down a little as it comes and up as it goes, fading either way, and the messages left move to their places; as a page comes, what is under its masthead fades in, rising a little, while the masthead stands still and nothing waits for the page before.
- Where the device asks for less motion, nothing slides, lifts or turns: what came with motion arrives at once (`base.css` cuts every animation and transition short), or only fades where it would be missed otherwise, as the points that rise and the menu of marks; and motion that would go on, as the waiting dots, stands still.

## The first game

- **Columns.** From 64 rem the game has three columns: the group chat, open all the time, and the groups; the board; the clue log and the tools, among them a match's progress, which opens in a sheet. The waiting room has two, each column stacked from the top. On a phone the groups, the clue log, the chat and a match's progress open as sheets from the action bar.
- **Points that rise** in a game ([../games/keywords.md](../games/keywords.md#points-as-they-come)): handwritten in `--font-hand`, in the group's deep shade, with no ground but a ring of paper drawn with shadows around each stroke, so that they read on a card of their own color; they rise a short way and fade, or only fade under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and let every tap through. On a card about a third of its height; big in the middle of the screen at the end.
- **Cards.** Hidden: white. The viewer's choice: yellow. Known but hidden: the tint, labelled in the corner. Revealed: the full color, pressed in; irrelevant papers struck through; Reviewer 2 ink with yellow type and a slanted stamp. A card revealed in front of the viewer turns over: its face turns away as it lay, white, the viewer's yellow or, for a PI or on a marked card, the tint it showed (Reviewer 2's ink for a PI), its color comes on the way back, and it lands pressed into the table; on Reviewer 2 the stamp slams down once the card has landed. Only a card revealed live turns: a board that catches up after a reload, a locked screen or a pause shows its cards as they lie, and a replay turns a card only on the step that reveals it. Where the device asks for less motion, the card is revealed at once. The others' choices hang as avatars on a card's corner; the card's accessible name says who they are.
- **Marks.** A group's marks, for its students and its PI alike: a round sticker hanging on the bottom right corner with a tick, a cross or an exclamation mark, which the card's accessible name says in words; for the students the card takes the tint of the color the mark stands for, the group's own, the other group's or `--r2-tint`, unless it is the viewer's choice, and for the PI, who only reads the marks, it keeps the tint of its own color. A student sets them from a menu that a long press on the card opens, or a right click (`MarkMenu.vue`), with no tools to pick, and a line under the board says what a tap and a press do; a long press selects no text and opens no menu of the browser's.
- **The clock** is red while the viewer has to act, a PI with a clue to give or a student with cards to reveal.

## The second game

- **Columns.** From 64 rem the game has two: the board, with what the viewer can do now under it, and a column beside it with the off-field chat, for those who may read it, the record of the rounds and the tools: a match's progress, in a match, and how to play. The waiting room has two, each stacked from the top. On a phone the board comes first, then what the viewer can do now, then the action bar: the record, how to play, the off-field chat and, in a match, its progress, which open as sheets; at the end the record's button reviews the game.
- **Numbers.** Once a game starts, everyone in it is shown by their number ([../games/impostor.md](../games/impostor.md#numbers)): an avatar with the number in ink on the color that the number chooses, as a guest's default avatar is drawn, so that a number has one color wherever it shows (`SeatAvatar.vue`). It opens the number's panel, with the hard shadow of every avatar that opens something, and a seat out of the game has it under the veil. The waiting room shows everyone's own avatar, as every room does, until the game starts.
- **Seat cards.** The board is a card for each number, two in a row on a phone and three from 40 rem, the same in every phase (`SeatCard.vue`): white, with the outline and no shadow, since only its avatar can be pressed; the avatar, how the seat stands as a tag, who sits there where the viewer may know it, as the list of who was who shows them at the end: the name, the AI's mark as an outlined pill and the role in small bold text, which the view carries for a seat that is out, for every seat with the god view and at the end; and the line the number wrote this round. The tags of the one writing now, of the viewer's own seat and of a seat out of the game are ink pills; "said" and "waiting" are grey words. A seat out of the game is grey and says how it went out. During a vote a card that the viewer may vote for is a choice: a tap anywhere on it but its avatar chooses it, and the chosen card turns yellow; the others are grey; and a card of its own after the seats is the choice to abstain.
- **The viewer's word** is a yellow sticker with the strong outline and no shadow, in the whose-turn strip, under the line on who the viewer is (`WordSticker.vue`, which also holds the first game's clue).
- **The record of the rounds** is a sheet on a phone and a panel beside the board on a wide screen: a fold for each round, which says on its right whom the round voted out and what they were, holding the round's lines, its votes and its reports (`RoundLog.vue`). Votes are rows (`VoteRows.vue`): a beige row for each number voted for, with its avatar, the count, and after an arrow the small avatars of those who voted so, the row that decided ending in an ink pill, "Out"; then a row for those who abstained and one for those whose time ran out, if any. The small avatars in a row open nothing and have no shadow.
- **A number's panel** is a sheet titled with the number, and the name where the viewer may know it: the big avatar, every line the number wrote by round, and the way to report it, a secondary button, which leads to a sheet of its own with the way back; the chair also has the danger button to remove whoever sits there, which asks first (`SeatPanel.vue`).
- **A report** opens a sheet for the whole room, with who reported whom, the line and the reason, and the count of those who have judged it (`JudgeSheet.vue`). Agree and disagree are two secondary buttons alike, which lean neither way. Once it is decided, the same sheet shows every verdict as the record shows votes, the row that decided ending in "Upheld".
- **The end.** After the letter, a sheet asks a player of a game with AI players which numbers they were, the numbers as cards to choose, with the avatar and the role, which turn yellow when chosen (`AgentGuess.vue`). Then who was who, a beige row for each number, the viewer's yellow, with the avatar, the name, the AI's outlined pill, the role, how their game went and the points in the ranking's colors (`RevealList.vue`), and the rating card. Each vote, as it is revealed during the game, is also a toast.
