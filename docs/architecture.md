# Architecture

How the app is put together. The constraints are in [rules/constraints.md](rules/constraints.md), the first game's rules in [games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md).

## One process, one port

One Node process serves everything on one port: the built front end, `/healthz`, the lists of thanks and the account API under `/api/`, avatars under `/avatars/` and Socket.IO under `/socket.io/`. Rooms live in memory and end with the process; feedback and accounts are in a SQLite database, avatars as files beside it ([Data](#data)). The only other things, serving no player, are the operator's status page, on a second port reachable only locally ([Status](#status)), and the mailer, a program of its own that alone holds the mail service's key and talks to the internet; the game server asks it over a Unix socket to send a mail, and needs no internet itself ([Mail](#mail)). The agent is another such program, which asks a language model for the moves of the AI players ([The agent](#the-agent)).

| Layer | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Shared | `shared/src/` | Types and rules both sides run; `schemas.ts` for the server alone |
| Server core | `server/src/core/` | Rooms, players, connections, limits, the word library |
| Server games | `server/src/games/<game>/` | One module per game: seats, rules, views |
| Server accounts | `server/src/accounts/` | Logins, the account API, email, avatars |
| Server store | `server/src/store/` | The database and what is kept in it |
| Mailer | `mailer/` | Sends the mails of accounts ([The mailer](#the-mailer)) |
| Agent | `agent/` | Asks a language model for the AI players' moves ([The agent](#the-agent)) |
| Client core | `client/src/core/` | Connection, rooms, lobby, language |
| Client games | `client/src/games/<game>/` | Each game's screens |

## Rooms

- **Codes:** four characters of Crockford's base32, shown as `R2-K7QX`, linked as `/room/K7QX`; `parseCode()` in `shared/src/codes.ts` reads them. Codes are reused, so every snapshot carries a random `roomId`.
- **Phases:** `lobby`, `playing`, `ended`, back to `lobby`. Each game has a random `gameId`. A room can be a [match](#matches).
- **The chair** is the first member and alone changes settings, starts and ends games and matches, shuffles, benches, removes, invites AI players ([AI players](#ai-players)), hands the chair on, or closes the room. When the chair leaves, or is offline for 3 minutes, the earliest-joined online member takes over, never an AI player.
- **Playing or watching.** A newcomer sends `play: true` to play; the game decides what that means ([seats](games/keywords.md#seats)). A returning member keeps their seat. A removed one stays out by their member key, which is the account's for a player who is logged in and the browser's otherwise, and by the keys of the browsers they were connected from: for as long as the room lasts (`removed`) if it was public when they were removed, and for 2 minutes (`removed_recently`) if it was private, whatever it becomes later. A room remembers its last 200 removals.
- **Revisions.** Every change bumps `rev`.
- **Screens,** such as a projector, watch without a token or seat, through `room:watch`, with a spectator's view; up to 5 per room. In a public room a screen takes an account with a seat there, one screen each ([Public rooms](#public-rooms)).

## Public rooms

- **Listing.** A room is private unless its creator makes it public, or the chair does in the waiting room (`room:public`). The home page lists public rooms (`meta:rooms`): code, game, language, fields, or that every game draws them anew, member count and whether a game is on; no full rooms or rooms with nobody online; at most 50, waiting rooms first, then the oldest.
- **Accounts.** Creating, listing or joining a public room, by the list, code or link, takes an account (`account_required`), so that players can be reported and barred. Guests and screens already in a room stay when it is listed. A new screen there takes an account with a seat (`not_member`), one each, a new one replacing the old.
- **Reports.** A member with an account may report another (`room:report`). The `reports` table (migration 6) keeps who reported whom, the name they had, the room's code and the note, for the status page and `admin.cjs reports list`; it is an archive ([Feedback](#feedback)).
- **Bars.** `admin.cjs accounts bar` keeps an account out of public rooms (`public_barred`), not private ones. A public room whose chair could no longer list it, a barred account or a guest, goes off the list by the next sweep; everyone in it stays.

## Players and identity

- **Tokens.** A browser gets a random 32-byte token on its first connection and keeps it in local storage. Every connection first gets `session`, with the server's `version`, the `account` logged in (or null) and, when just issued, the `token`.
- **Member keys.** The server never stores tokens; it works with the token's SHA-256, which never leaves the server. An account's key is the SHA-256 of its ID, so an account is one player on every device.
- **Names.** A member with an account goes by its username. A guest gives no name: the room numbers its guests in the order they first come in, from 1 (`PlayerView.guest`), never giving a number twice, and a guest who leaves and comes back with the same member key has their number again, for as long as the room remembers the key (the last 500, `MAX_GUESTS`). The client calls a guest "Guest 3", in the interface's language, with the first character of that word as the avatar (`client/src/core/names.ts`); the server's own name for them, in what it says itself, such as a report, is the English one. A guest who logs in leaves the number behind. No username may be a guest's name, in either language, or one of the reserved names (`isReservedName` in `shared/src/accounts.ts`). The rules for names that people write, such as a username, are in `shared/src/names.ts` and `shared/src/text.ts`, shared with the client.

## Messages

Everything after the page load goes over Socket.IO, except the account API ([Accounts](#accounts)). `shared/src/protocol.ts` describes the room messages; each game adds `<game>:<action>` ones.

- **Answers:** `{ ok: true, ... }` or `{ ok: false, error }`; an unknown message is `bad_request`. The client shows `errors.<code>` from the locale files.
- **Validation:** every payload against a zod schema from `shared/src/schemas.ts`, which the client never imports (ESLint refuses it): zod probes `new Function`, which the Content Security Policy forbids.
- **In order:** one connection's messages run in arrival order, and handlers never await between reading a room and writing it.
- **Snapshots.** After every visible change, each connection gets its own complete `room:state`; the client keeps the highest `rev` for its `roomId`.
- **Closing.** `room:closed` says why a room is gone: `removed` or `removed_recently`, as `room:join` would then answer, `dismissed` (a screen the chair sent away), `expired`, `left`, `closed` or `restart`.

## The handshake and reconnecting

The handshake carries `{ token, code, roomId }` (a screen `{ watch: code, roomId }`), and a member's connection is put back into its room before any message runs. It only brings back: its one answer is `room:closed` with `expired` for a `roomId` the code no longer has, so the client always follows it with `room:join` or `room:watch`.

The home page asks `meta:seats` once connected, after every reconnect and when it hears `room:closed` (a connection that came from a room's page is still in it), and offers a way back to each room where the connection still has a member's seat, never taking the player there by itself. Seats are found by the connection's key, its account's on every device, or by the guest seat its account would adopt; the answer gives each room's code, game and whether a game is on (`playing` until the room is back in the waiting room), at most 5, the newest first. It looks up no code, so it doesn't count against an address's codes.

## Games

A game implements `GameModule` (`server/src/core/game.ts`). The core owns the room, its members and the chair; the module owns its data and decides seats, settings, starting, messages, deadlines, views and matches. Its data is immutable. `views()` is the only way its data leaves the server to players, called once per snapshot for each member and the screens. A module also says what the record of a game keeps of it ([Game records](#game-records)).

**Words.** The library also reads the English meanings of the Chinese characters of its lists, from `words/dictionary/han-glosses.json`, which the cards carry for the clue check ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#the-clue)); without the file it has none, which the line `word_lists_loaded` says (`glossed`). The room draws from the chair's fields, unused words first. A room may instead draw its fields anew for every game (`randomFields`): at each start every field with enough words in the room's language for a game of its own goes in on the toss of a coin, and one of them at random when none does (`drawFields()` in `shared/src/protocol.ts`, which the room setup's "choose at random" uses too). It then needs no field chosen, and keeps those chosen for when the chair turns it off. Nobody is told which fields a game drew but its record (`fields`); the public list says that the room draws them. A module may say which words must not share a game, with `wordsClash()` ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#setting-up)). `drawWords()` in `server/src/core/words.ts` takes the words in random order and keeps each that clashes with none kept before; `scripts/check-words.mjs` makes sure each list has enough such words ([../words/README.md](../words/README.md#contributing)). If short, it draws again, each time the word with the fewest clashes; when some clash all the same, the game starts and the server logs `words_clashed`.

**Time.** A module says when it next has something to do with `deadline()`. The server keeps one timer per room and calls `tick()` when it fires and before every game message, so an action for a turn whose time ran out is `stale` even before the timer fired.

**Errors.** A module that throws leaves the room as it was; the message is answered `internal` and logged.

## AI players

The chair invites Reviewer 1 (`AGENT_PERSONAS` in `shared/src/agent.ts`) to a group's seat with `room:addAgent`, as its PI or one of its students, where it plays like anyone, through the rules. A group has one AI PI and one AI student at most (`agent_limit`). While they are tried out:

- **Who may.** Only a chair whose account the operator lets have them (`admin.cjs agents allow`, the `agents` column of `accounts`, migration 15), or the chair of a [practice room](#practice-rooms), in a private room that plays no match, on a server with an agent (`AGENT_SOCKET`); `RoomView.agents` says whether the chair may now, and the client shows nothing of them otherwise. Elsewhere `agents_not_allowed`. A room with AI players can't be listed or play a match (`agents_in_room`). Those in a room stay when its chair could no longer invite them.
- **Members.** An AI player is a member with a persona (`PlayerView.agent`), which the client names it by, and no connection, browser or account. It counts toward the room's 30, never chairs (`player_is_agent`), doesn't watch, and keeps no room alive: the AI players go with the last person, and a room where only they are online closes as one with nobody online does. The chair removes one with `room:remove`, or with `room:spectate`, which removes it just the same, and may invite it again; the shuffle leaves its seat alone. It is online while the agent answers, so a game can't start with an AI PI offline (`captain_offline`).
- **Before a game.** An AI player can be invited once a person sits in a group (`needs_human`, as a refusal and as a start problem), so that a room is never the AI players' alone, and with AI players seated every time limit must be on (`needs_timers`), so that a slow or missing agent never holds a game up.
- **Playing** (`server/src/core/agents.ts`). After every change, the driver asks the game what each AI seat is to do (`GameModule.agentTask`), asks the agent with that seat's own view, and sends the messages that the answer makes (`GameModule.agentMove`) through `Room.act()`, at a pace that people can follow. An answer for a moment that has passed is dropped, and no AI plays on in a game that every person has been offline in for 20 seconds; until then, the one person in a room may reload without an AI dropping what it was doing. A request that brings no answer (`busy`, `no_answer`, `no_legal_clue`) is made again after 10 seconds, then after twice as long each time up to a minute, for as long as the phase lasts. When the agent can't be reached, or its model's service is gone (`model_failed`), the agent is down (`agent_down`) and its players are offline until a ping every 30 seconds finds it back (`agent_up`); the clocks carry the games on meanwhile.
- **Playing for someone offline.** In a room where the chair may invite AI players, a person who has been offline for 20 seconds while it is their turn is played for by an AI, unless a person of their group can play the turn; until then they count as there, so that a reload or a locked phone changes nothing of what any AI does. One AI plays the guesses of a group: its AI student, if it has one, or else one of its offline students. Whoever is played for is marked (`PlayerView.takenOver`) from the moment an AI is set to play for them, as it is asked what to do, until they are back, when it stops at once.
- **Points.** A game in which an AI played, as a player or for someone offline, is on the record as any game, marked (`agents` in `game_records`, migration 15), but brings nobody points, a streak, a [title](#titles) or a [friend title](#friend-duos).
- **The record** names the AI players (`agents` in the log), marks every move made for someone offline with the persona (`agent`), and keeps the AI's own log beside the game's ([Game records](#game-records)).
- **Its page.** Anyone may open an AI player's page in a room, from its avatar (`AgentProfileCard.vue`, in its panel; `GET /api/agent/<persona>`, `AgentProfileView`): player number 0, which no account has, a line about it, how many of it are in the rooms now, as AI players and playing for someone offline, and in how many rooms, counted as the page is read (`agentsIn` in `server/src/core/agents.ts`); and what it has done in the games that finished, as counts alone: games and wins, as a PI and as a student, the cards its clues found per clue, the clue that found the most, how often it revealed Reviewer 2, and the games it played in for someone offline. An account also sees, apart, what it has played with it: the games in its group and against it, won and lost, and those it was played for in. The counts come from `agent_plays` (migration 15), a row for each AI player in a seat at the end of a game that finished, with what the game counts of it (`GameModule.agentFeats`), and one for each person it played for, with their account, which is forgotten when the account is deleted. It is in no ranking and has no friends.

## Practice rooms

Anyone with an account may practice a game with Reviewer 1 from the home page: "Practice with Reviewer 1" in the first game's card opens a sheet with the role, the language and the word lists, folded into a line that names them. The role and the lists are those of the account's last practice room ([the last room setup](#accounts)), or of the browser's from before, kept in local storage, or the PI and the general list. `room:create` with `practice` and the role, as the game names it, makes a private room (`Room.practice`) with the game's default settings, which plays no match: the creator sits where the game puts them for that role, Reviewer 1 takes the other seats (`GameModule.practice`, for the first game the red group's other role and the blue group's two), and the game starts once the creator is in the room. A guest is refused (`account_required`), whom the home page asks to log in or register; so are a role that the game doesn't have and `practice` with `match`, `public`, `settings` or `randomFields` (`bad_request`). While the agent doesn't answer, there are none (`agents_offline`): the home page asks with `meta:agents`, when it opens and when the sheet does, and greys the button out; on a server without an agent, which answers `available: null`, it shows no button at all. Nothing asks the agent while no AI plays, so `meta:agents` answers what the agent said last and, if that was more than 30 seconds ago (`FRESH_MS`), asks it again for the next answer (`AgentDriver.recheck`): a model that went away meanwhile is found out before the player has picked a role. A refusal leaves no room behind.

After its first game a practice room is a private room like any other, which the chair may invite AI players to, whatever the chair's account, and where an AI plays for someone offline. It stays one whoever chairs it. Each of its games says so in its record (`practice`, migration 18), as a fact of the game: whether AI players were still in it is in `agents`. There is no limit of its own on how many there may be at once; the agent's queue (`AGENT_QUEUE`) is the limit there is.

## Replays

The players with accounts can watch a game that finished again, and nothing of it is kept apart: `POST /api/account/replay` with `gameId` works the replay out from the [record of the game](#game-records), how it began and its log, as the game says (`GameModule.showReplay`), and sends it, keeping nothing. A replay belongs to the game, not to an account: it goes to an account that has the game in `game_records`, and `no_replay` to anyone else, as for a game that didn't finish, one whose log grew too long to keep, and one of a room with the chair's own words, which is not kept at all ([custom words](games/keywords.md#custom-words)). Deleting an account deletes its `game_records`, and the replay stays for the others who played in it.

**A replay keeps no name.** For the first game, the server plays the game through again from how it began, the board with every color, the group that started and the settings, with every move in its log, by the functions of the rules that made them (the `apply` functions of `server/src/games/keywords/rules.ts`), which check nothing: the log holds only the moves that the rules took, under the rules of their time; what a move was, such as one in the last seconds of its phase, is as the rules say now. That gives the clues and the guesses, who gave each clue and revealed each card, by player ID, when each came, the cards each PI meant, every turn whose time ran out, with the PI it cost, and the result; and the two groups as the game ended, each PI and its students by player ID, from where everyone sat as it began and every seat taken, changed or left during it, in order. The answer is the replay as it is shown (`ShownReplay`), with who everyone in the groups and the record was: an account by its username, avatar and handle as they are now, from `game_players`, an account deleted since as a deleted player, by the log's `deleted`, a guest by their number in the room and an AI player by its persona, as the log names them, and the player ID that the account asking had, which its group's bar shows first. The page shows a replay with the groups' bars and lets anyone open a panel from an avatar there, with the moves of that player ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#replays)).

## Game records

The record of a game (migrations 12 and 15) is one row in the `replays` table, on the server alone: no API, page or command sends it anywhere but the replay worked out from it, which goes to the accounts that played in a finished game ([Replays](#replays)). Every game is kept, however it ended and whoever played in it, guests alone included, except a game of a room with the chair's own words, whose words stay with the room ([custom words](games/keywords.md#custom-words)). The records are for training and testing the AI players and for research, so each says what kind of game it was, as facts of the game alone; whether a game is of use for some study, such as one that someone abandoned at once, is for whoever reads the records to decide, by rules of their own, which they can change and run again.

- **How it ended** (`ending`), set by the room: `finished`, by itself, such as when a group won; `withdrawn` by the chair, alone or with the match that the chair ended; `abandoned`, cut short when its room closed, whose log says why (`end` with `why`: `chair` when the chair closed the room, `empty` when its last person left, `idle` or `stale` when nobody was online, or nothing happened, in it for too long); or `interrupted`, cut short when the server stopped. Only a finished game has results, points, streaks, titles and a replay, and only it says who won (`winner`) and how (`reason`), in the game's own words (`GameModule.outcome`: for the first game, the group, and `found` when it found every card of its own or `reviewer2` when the other group revealed Reviewer 2).
- **What kind of game it was:** when it began (`started_at`) and ended (`ended_at`); how it began (`setup`, as JSON, `GameModule.setup`: for the first game, the language, the settings, the group that started and the board with every color); how many played in it, by member key, those who left included, with an account (`accounts`), as guests (`guests`) and as AI players (`agents`); whether an AI played for someone who was offline (`takeover`); whether it was a game of a match (`in_match`); whether it was played in a [practice room](#practice-rooms) (`practice`, migration 18); its language and fields (`fields`, as JSON); and the versions of the server (`version`, `APP_VERSION`), of the game's rules (`rules`, `GameModule.rulesVersion`, which goes up with every change to the rules or the scoring) and of the word lists (`words`, the first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the lists as read). A game kept has every one of these, but `setup` and `rules` where the game says nothing of them, and only a finished game has a `winner` and a `reason` (migration 22).
- **The log** (`server/src/core/gameLog.ts`, `log`): everything that changed the game, in order, from its start to its end. Each entry says when, in milliseconds from the start on the room's clock; who, by the player ID they had in the room, or nobody for the room itself; and what: one of the game's messages, as the game says to keep it (`GameModule.logged`), what the game keeps of a change of its own (`GameModule.logChanges`), such as how a group's marks stood when it revealed a card, when a turn ended and when the game ended, or one of the room's own events: joining and leaving, the chair benching or removing a player, the chair passing to someone else, an AI starting and stopping play for someone who is offline (`takeover`), a timer that changed the game, with those it was waiting for (`GameModule.due`) who were offline then, and the end, which for a game that didn't finish says how. It begins with where everyone in the room was, as the game describes each of them (`GameModule.seated`), which also says where the game put whoever came during it, and names the AI players among them by persona (`agents`) and every guest who played by their number in the room (`guests`, by player ID). **What a player said** in their group's chat is in it, apart, as the entry's `said`, so that whoever reads the records can leave it out, and so is what they said after the end, as the groups talk the game over: the log goes on after its `end` with the lines of the chat alone, until the room leaves the game behind, when the chair plays again or starts the next match, or the room closes, and those lines are then added to the kept log (`Room.review()`), with the accounts of player IDs that came after the end, but those of an account deleted meanwhile. Not in it: who went offline or came back, quick reactions, refused messages, those that changed nothing, and what the game leaves out, such as cards chosen and chosen again, and each mark that a group sets and changes. Every room holds its log in memory while its game is on, so past 48 KB of chat it keeps no more of it, and notes once that it left it out (`truncated`); everything else, which says who sat where and what they did, is kept until a log past 128 KB is dropped (`game_log_too_large`), with the rest of the record kept. A whole game makes a few kilobytes.
- **The AI log** (`server/src/core/agentLog.ts`; `agent_log` in `replays`, migration 15): for each step of an AI ([AI players](#ai-players)), when it was asked, the seat, the persona and whether it played for someone offline, the task, either the answer with the agent's note (the model, the version of the prompts, how long it took, the clues it considered with what the rules said of them, and its reason) or why there was none, and whether the answer came too late to be of use (`late`). It has a limit of 64 KB of its own; past it, the log keeps no more and says so (`truncated`). A game that no AI played in has none, and an answer that comes after the end of its game goes into no log.
- **Who was who**: `game_players` links each player ID in the game to an account, for everyone with an account who played in it, those who left included, and one who left and came back under both IDs. Guests have no row. An account's ID never changes, and a deleted account's is never given out again, so a new username changes nothing here.
- **Deleting** an account marks the player IDs it had in the games it played in, in each log's `deleted`, in the same transaction, and keeps what it said, as nobody's, as the rest of the log is: nothing links the IDs to the account any more, and the mark lets whoever reads the records tell what deleted accounts said from what guests said, as for data that is made public. Then its rows in `game_players` go with it ([Replays](#replays)). An account deleted during a game counts as having played no more, and its player IDs are marked as the game is kept, as are the IDs of one deleted before what the groups said after the end is kept. The log still names the player IDs the account had, and a replay shows it as a deleted player.

The game says what its messages keep: for the first game, every message but choosing a card, as its schema reads it, without the game's ID and with the clue's word as the record keeps it, and a line of the chat as what the player said; of a group's marks, how they stood when it revealed a card, when a turn ended and when the game ended ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#replays)).

## Matches

A match plays a set number of games, scores every player in each, and ranks them by total. `MatchRules` (`shared/src/match.ts`), given at creation: 1 to 10 games (3); groups `fixed` or `switch`, made `random` by the server or by `choose` of the players. The room keeps the match (`server/src/core/match.ts`); the game scores and groups it through `GameModule.match` ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#matches)). A withdrawn game isn't one of the match's. Settings are locked once a game has been scored. After the last scored game, the players still in the room are ranked by total, ties sharing a place: the highest total gets the Best Paper Award, and with three or more ranked, the lowest gets Academic Trash, `black_hole` in the code and the database. Ending early ranks as they stand, without titles. Each ranked account keeps the match (`TitleStore` in `server/src/store/records.ts`).

## Limits and housekeeping

- **Rate:** about 10 messages a second per connection, bursts of 20; a message is at most 16 KB.
- **Per address:** 5 rooms a minute (`ROOMS_PER_MINUTE`), 20 codes a minute that belong to no room (past them, public rooms are still found), 3 pieces of feedback every 10 minutes (`FEEDBACK_PER_10_MINUTES`). Behind a reverse proxy, `TRUST_PROXY` takes the address from `X-Forwarded-For`. **Per account:** 5 reports an hour, 30 friend requests a day, 30 searches for an account by username an hour, one invitation a minute to the same friend ([Friends](#friends)), 100 likes a day ([Public profiles](#public-profiles)).
- **In one place:** `SocketLimits` in `server/src/core/socket.ts`, `RoomLimits` in `server/src/core/rooms.ts`. 30 players per room, 2000 rooms per server.
- **The sweep,** once a minute, closes rooms with nobody online for 30 minutes or nothing happening for 6 hours, and drops members offline for 10 minutes from waiting rooms.
- **Stopping:** every connection hears `room:closed` with `restart`, then the database closes; after 5 seconds the process ends anyway (`stop_timed_out`). A wrong setting, library or database stops the server before it listens (`startup_failed`, `listen_failed`).

## HTTP

Files under `/assets/` carry a content hash and are cached for good; everything else is `no-cache`. Every response carries a Content Security Policy (`script-src 'self'`, no framing) and `nosniff`.

## Data

- **`DATA_DIR`** ([development.md](development.md)) holds `reviewer2.db`, `snapshots/` and `avatars/`. Without it, the database is in memory, gone at every stop.
- **SQLite** through `node:sqlite`, synchronous; WAL mode, a 5-second wait on a lock, secure delete.
- **Migrations:** `PRAGMA user_version` and `server/src/store/migrations.ts`, run on start in one transaction, after a snapshot.
- **Snapshots** every hour into `snapshots/`, the last three kept.
- **The disk.** Feedback stops at 10,000 pieces or 50 MB, which `MAX_FEEDBACK` raises together; nothing is deleted to make room, and only what the operator puts in the trash ever goes, 30 days later ([Feedback](#feedback)). Past 1 GB nothing new is kept (`storage_full`). Avatars up to 2 GB.
- **Deleting.** Everything of an account goes with it through foreign keys, its friendships, its likes, given and received, and its rows in `game_players` included; earlier snapshots keep it. Its feedback, its contributions among it, which show a deleted player where it signed them ([Feedback contributions](#feedback-contributions)), and the reports it sent or got stay, no longer linked to it, and so do the records of the games it played in, the AI's logs among them, which hold the clues, every move by player ID and what it said in the chat, marked as an account's that was deleted, since the others played in them too, but not its name ([Replays](#replays), [Game records](#game-records)).

## Feedback

Players send it from the foot of the home page or a room; the status page shows it. The rules are in `shared/src/feedback.ts`, run on both sides. **Kept:** text, contact, kind (a suggestion, a new game, a bug or the word lists), version, language, page, time, for a bug only the user agent, and who it is from, in one of three ways ([Feedback contributions](#feedback-contributions)): signed with the account the player was logged in to (`link_profile`), which keeps the account and no name; anonymous, with the account if they were logged in; or a guest's name of their own. The form asks whether to sign the feedback, which it offers first, or to stay anonymous: a player who is logged in signs with the account, and a name that an older page sends along signs with it too and isn't kept; a guest types a name of their own. The form says that what a player writes may become a public GitHub issue, never their contact. Feedback from before also kept a link for the name and whether the player agreed to a public issue, which migration 17 dropped. **Not kept:** address, nickname, token, member key, room code. When full, players are told (`feedback_full`).

**An archive, with a trash.** The feedback is the project's archive of suggestions: no code changes what a player sent, and `server/src/store/archive.test.ts` fails if any is added. The only changes are what players were promised and the operator's notes: **contacts are cleared after 90 days** (`contacts_cleared`), deleting an account unlinks its feedback, and `admin.cjs feedback` records what became of it (migration 9), which the status page shows as a status and a trail: `issue` the GitHub issue it became, with its text and who sent it and never its contact; `resolve` the pull request that resolved it; `close` and `reopen` whether it was closed without being adopted, which adopted feedback can't be; `adopt` makes an issue that isn't closed a contribution ([Feedback contributions](#feedback-contributions)), and `describe` rewrites what the contribution says. The status is the first of these that holds: in the trash, closed, resolved, which is adopted, whether a pull request was recorded or not, an issue, which is published as an issue or recorded as resolved by a pull request without being adopted, or new. Its badge writes out what the feedback came to, such as "Issue #42", "Resolved · PR #72 · issue #42", or "Issue #42 · PR #72" for feedback whose pull request was recorded but which still waits to be adopted. The page's views, in one row, each with its count, are all, the trash included until it is purged, then open, which is the new feedback that nobody has dealt with yet and the page's default, and each other status alone, issued, resolved, closed and trash; none overlaps another but all, and all of them list the latest first. The issue's text comes from `feedback draft`, which writes its title and body from a template, with the player's text and who sent it alone, as the form promises: the username of the account that signed it as it is then, or a deleted player once it is gone, a guest's name, or an anonymous player, for the operator to create the issue on a machine that reaches GitHub; the server never talks to GitHub. `feedback delete` puts feedback in the trash and `feedback restore` brings it back; the server deletes what has been in the trash for 30 days, at start and every hour, logging only the count (`feedback_purged`). That purge is the one way a row ever goes, and it leaves feedback that was adopted or is a GitHub issue alone, however it got to the trash: adopted feedback can't be deleted, since it is a contribution, nor can feedback that is a GitHub issue, which is public and is closed instead, and feedback in the trash still counts against the limits until it is purged. The table was rebuilt once, in migration 5, every row copied with its id, so that its kinds are checked in `shared/src/feedback.ts` alone and a new kind needs no migration; since then, migrations only add to it, but for migration 17, which dropped two columns that nothing used any more ([Feedback contributions](#feedback-contributions)). The reports of players are a strict archive: no row is deleted ([Public rooms](#public-rooms)).

## Feedback contributions

The players whose feedback was adopted are listed on the home page from the database, and nowhere else in the repository: a contribution is a row of the feedback that `admin.cjs feedback adopt` adopted, of the kind of the feedback (a suggestion, a new game, a bug or the word lists). Every adopted piece of feedback is a GitHub issue first, and `adopt` refuses one that isn't; the issue, which links the pull requests that closed it, is its record on GitHub. Adopting records when it was adopted and one sentence in Chinese and one in English on what the player contributed, written by the operator; `feedback describe` rewrites the two, and nothing else of a contribution ever changes (migration 17). `shared/src/contributions.ts` has the rules.

`GET /api/contributions` lists them, the newest first, each with its kind, who it is from, the sentences, the issue and the month it was adopted, never the text, the contact or the account. Feedback signed with an account (`link_profile`) keeps no name: it is listed by the account as it is now, its username, the handle of its public profile and its avatar, which the home page shows before the name, both opening the account's panel ([Public profiles](#public-profiles)), and once the account is deleted as a deleted player's (`deleted`), by no name, avatar or link. A guest's is listed by the name they left, and anonymous feedback by none; neither holds a trace of an account. The home page keeps a column for the avatar on the entries that have one, and what follows the name wraps under itself. An account's page counts its contributions of each kind, anonymous ones too, and its public profile does while its owner shows them; deleting the account unlinks them, and they stay on the home page.

## Acknowledgments

`ACKNOWLEDGMENTS.md` holds the maintainers' own thanks, written by hand as they like, in any language, to anyone, with an account or without, and the home page shows it ([Lists of thanks](#lists-of-thanks)). It is a little Markdown, which the page shows as GitHub does: headings, paragraphs, lists, `https://` links, bold and italic ([format](development.md#acknowledgments)). `shared/src/acknowledgments.ts` reads it into blocks of plain text, which the page builds its own elements from, never HTML. It refuses HTML, character references, images, horizontal rules, bare web addresses, links that aren't `https://` and links inside bold or italic text, in CI and on the server; anything else it makes nothing of, such as a numbered list, shows as it is written, which may differ from GitHub.

Until migration 17, an `acknowledgments` table recorded whom the operator thanked, by adopting feedback or by hand, and the file listed every adopted piece of feedback. The migration made the adopted feedback contributions, adopted when its row said, and dropped the table; those thanked by hand are in the file alone.

## Sponsors

`SPONSORS.md` thanks the sponsors by name: no amounts, tiers, or whether they give monthly. Every day, `.github/workflows/sponsors.yml` asks GitHub for the maintainer's public sponsors, ended ones too, the oldest first, and writes their usernames, linked to their profiles, between the two lines that mark the section `GitHub Sponsors` (`scripts/update-sponsors.mjs`); a sponsor who makes their sponsorship private drops off at the next run. A change goes to a PR of the maintainer's own, which merges without a version once CI passes. Maintainers add those who sponsor in other ways, such as a WeChat tip, under `Other ways`, and only if they ask, by a name that follows the nickname rules, perhaps with an `https://` link, and perhaps with a line of thanks of the maintainers' own to them after ` — `, in any language, which the home page shows under the name as plain text, untranslated ([format](development.md#sponsors)). Whether a sponsor has one has nothing to do with what they gave. `shared/src/sponsors.ts` has the rules.

## Lists of thanks

The home page shows the feedback contributions, the sponsors and the acknowledgments as three folded cards, the first two with titles that count the people listed, so it asks `GET /api/contributions`, `GET /api/sponsors` and `GET /api/acknowledgments` once, when it shows them, and again when a card that failed to load is opened. The contributions come from the database ([Feedback contributions](#feedback-contributions)); the other two are files. The build bundles both files into the server. With `CONTENT_DIR`, the server shows the copies in that directory instead, so a list changes without a new build or a restart: each request compares the file's modification time, size and inode with those of the copy read last, and the server reads the file again only when they differ, or when it couldn't open or read it the last time, such as for its permissions. A file that is missing, over 64 KiB, not UTF-8 or against the rules leaves the bundled copy in use, and the log says why with `thanks_list_skipped`, once for each new reason. The server never writes to the directory. The same parsers check both files on the server and in CI (`server/src/thanks.test.ts`): `shared/src/acknowledgments.ts` the acknowledgments, `shared/src/lists.ts` and `shared/src/sponsors.ts` the sponsors. Everything in them shows as plain text; links, only `https://`, open in a new tab with `rel="nofollow ugc noopener"`.

## Accounts

Optional: a username and a password, with an avatar, a record of games, titles, the count of its [feedback contributions](#feedback-contributions), a [public profile](#public-profiles), and, if wanted, an email address for resetting a forgotten password, which itself is never kept.

- **Usernames** follow the nickname rules, with at least 2 characters, no reserved names and no guest's name ([Players and identity](#players-and-identity)); `usernameKey()` in `shared/src/accounts.ts` compares them.
- **Passwords:** 8 to 128 characters after NFKC, as scrypt hashes, N = 2^15, r = 8, p = 3 (`server/src/accounts/passwords.ts`). Two hashes at a time, then `busy`.
- **Logins:** a 32-byte secret in a cookie, kept as its SHA-256 (`sessions`). `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/`, and unless `COOKIE_SECURE=0`, `Secure` with `__Host-`. Ends after 30 days unused, a year in all, or on logout or a password change; its connections then get `account:ended`.
- **Connections.** The handshake reads the cookie; a logged-in connection takes its member key from the account. A guest's seat becomes the account's when the browser logs in and comes back.
- **Records.** At a game's end the room asks `GameModule.results()` and keeps the result per account, with the game's points ([Academic ranks](#academic-ranks)), the titles it brought the player ([Titles](#titles)), the streak it brings ([Streaks](#streaks)) and the titles it brings friends ([Friend duos](#friend-duos)). A game of a match keeps the match's ID, its number in the match and how many games the match was to have (`Room.matchGame()`, migration 14). The account page shows four numbers on small blocks, the games of every kind, the share of them won, as a whole percentage rounded down, how many matches the account finished, and how many of them it finished first, level with others too (`RecordCounts`, `RecordStats.vue`); the server still sends the games outside matches and those in matches apart. Then the latest 20 games, one line each: when it ended, in numbers as 2026.10.14 18:08 in every language, whether it was an ordinary game or which of its match, as "Match 2/3", or, for a game that an AI played in, which is never one of a match, the robot's head and "AI" in their place, which a tap explains: it brought nobody points (`GameRecord.agents`), the role on a chip in the group's tint, the decision by its short English name in every language ("Oral", "Desk Reject"), and a round button on the right to watch it again; and the latest 20 matches, each with its games, its place and total, and its title on the right. The columns of each list line up from line to line where the browser has subgrids.
- **The last room setup.** As an account makes a room, the server keeps how it set it up (`RoomSetup` in `shared/src/accounts.ts`, `room_setup` as JSON, migration 19): the game, the room's language, the fields and whether every game draws them anew, the game's settings as the game took them, the match's rules, whether the room is public and whether the creator plays; and, for a practice room, the role and the fields, apart. The owner's summary has it, and the next setup starts from it, as far as it still holds: fields with too few words in the language drop out, a setting that no longer will do is the game's default, as is one that the language counts in other units, such as a clue's length, and a room is public only for an account. A guest's last setup stays in the browser's local storage. It goes with the account.
- **Numbers.** An account's id, from 1 in the order of registration, never given out again, is its number, shown on its own page and its public profile and never a way to look an account up. The first account is the author's (`AUTHOR_ACCOUNT`), which both pages mark.

## Friends

Accounts only; guests see none of it. `shared/src/friends.ts` has the types, the limits and the error codes.

- **Search.** The account page finds someone by their whole username (`friends:find`), compared as names are, which answers with the handle of their profile and the username, or `no_such_account`; nothing finds an account by a part of a name or by its number. A link to a page, pasted with any text around it (`handleInLink` in `shared/src/accounts.ts`), is read as the page it leads to (`GET /api/profile/<handle>`), with no search. Either opens the other's panel (`ProfilePanel.vue`), as a tap on their avatar does: a brief card, under it what the account may do by how the two stand, and last the whole page. The owner's account page says that others find it so.
- **By how two stand.** A room's player panel, the search's panel and a public page offer the same (`FriendActions.vue`, by `standingOf` in `client/src/core/friends.ts` over the viewer's own friends list): a stranger is asked in a sheet with a line for them (`BefriendSheet.vue`); whoever was asked already, a word that the request went; whoever asked the account, their line and Accept and Decline; a friend, a name of the account's own (`AliasSheet.vue`) and the end of the friendship, after asking first (`UnfriendSheet.vue`), as from the friend's card on the account page. A page has them as small buttons beside the like, with the line of a request above them. The sheets lie over the panel or the page.
- **Requests.** An account asks another by username (`friends:request`), from the search's panel or from their public page, or a player in the same room by their ID from the player's panel, if both have accounts, with a line for them if it likes (`note`): one line of at most 50 characters, cleaned as a line of the chat is and otherwise as written (`checkFriendNote` in `shared/src/friends.ts`). The line goes to the account asked alone, on its account page, and is deleted from the database once the request is answered, accepted or declined, or withdrawn; a request asked anew may have a line of its own. The other side accepts or declines on their account page, or in the panel or on the page of whoever asked; asking someone who has asked already accepts theirs. Either side ends a friendship (`friends:remove`), which also takes back a request. Every change tells both sides' connections `friends:changed`, and so do a new username or avatar and the deletion of an account, to everyone whose page names it.
- **Limits** (`FRIEND_LIMITS`): 100 friends; 20 requests open per sender and 50 waiting per account; 30 requests a day per account, counted before a username is looked up, so that trying names costs as much as asking, and for a player in the room only when a request goes out; 30 searches an hour per account (`findsPerHour`, `friend_find_limited`), counted before the name is looked up, so that a name that finds nobody costs as much as one that finds someone, and none for a link, which leads to a page that anyone with the link may open anyway; after a decline, whoever was declined waits 7 days before asking again, while whoever declined may ask at any time. The other friends messages answer a name that is no account's as they answer a stranger's.
- **Names for friends.** An account may give each friend a name of its own (`friends:alias`, `checkAlias` in `shared/src/friends.ts`), for itself alone, as a phone's contacts have: one line of at most 20 characters, cleaned as a nickname is, which an empty one takes away. The friend isn't told, and nobody else is sent it: it goes in the account's own friends list (`FriendView.alias`) and with a friend's invitations to it (`InvitationView.from.alias`). The account's pages call the friend by it wherever they name them, in place of the username, as a phone's contacts do: on the account page and where the account invites friends, in a room and its games, in replays, among the feedback contributions and among those who liked the account's page (`nameOf` in `client/src/core/names.ts`). A friend's panel, their whole page, a shared link to it and the questions before removing, reporting or parting from them put the username after it in small grey type, since such a name may be anyone's. A room's big screen, which the whole room sees, keeps to usernames, whoever is logged in on it, and a default avatar is drawn from the username all the same. The client reads the names from the friends list as each connection of an account is made and on every `friends:changed`, so that a new name shows at once; the server never puts them into a room's view.
- **Presence.** `friends:list` says of each friend whether they are online, every tab and device counting as one person, and which room they are in: with its code if the room is public, so that the page offers a Join button, or if the account has a seat there too, and without it if the room is private, which only an invitation leads into. Nobody but a friend learns this, through any message or API; requests show the username and avatar alone. The account page shows it on each friend's card: a green dot on the avatar of a friend who is online, the whole card faded for one who isn't, and on the name's line the way into the room, "Join room" for a public room and "Ask to join room" for a private one, never the words of where they are. A profile and a panel show nobody's presence.
- **Friends playing.** The home page, for an account, lists the rooms that its friends are in and it isn't (`friends:rooms`, `FriendRoomView`): a row a room, with the friends in it, by the account's names for them, and whether a game is on, and no row while no friend is in a room. The list is asked for as the page opens, on every connection, on `friends:changed` and when the page comes back into sight. A public room has its Join link; a private one, still without its code, an Ask to join button, which asks the first friend in the row, as the button on a friend's card on the account page asks that friend.
- **Invitations.** A member with an account invites a friend into their room (`friends:invite`), online or not, from the start bar at the top of the waiting room, beside the start button for the chair, or from the room menu during a game and while a finished match's ranking stands in the waiting room, once a minute for the same friend, unless the chair has removed the friend from it. The sheet lists every friend who isn't in the room, those online first, with the friends list's green dot, and those offline after them, faded, whose button says that they will see the invitation once online. Every connection of the friend hears `friends:invited`, with the inviter, the room's code and ID and how long the invitation stands, and a connection that comes while it stands hears it too. `room:join` with the `invitation` in place of the code lets the friend in, into a private room too, as a newcomer like any other: a removal by the chair and the account requirement of a public room keep them out as they would anyone. An invitation is used up when the friend comes into the room, by it or by the code, and forgotten when they ignore it (`friends:ignore`); it lapses after 10 minutes, when the inviter leaves, when the room closes or when the two part (`invitation_expired`). One that lapses by its age ends on a timer, as it lapses; one that no longer stands for another reason is found by the sweep. However an invitation ends, every connection of the friend hears `friends:uninvited`, so that none of their pages shows it on. The inviter's own view of the room alone lists the friends whom they invited and who haven't come, while the invitations stand, the oldest first (`RoomView.invited`), and gets the room again as one ends: the start bar shows them as avatars beside its line, as someone the room waits for, and one who comes in in their own colors for a moment, the dots stilled, before they go; the sheet says "Invited" for each. Whoever has a seat in the room comes back by the code, with a lapsed invitation in hand or not. Invitations live in memory and are never kept.
- **Asking to be let in.** An account asks a friend who is in a room to be let in (`friends:ask`), and learns nothing of the room by asking. Every connection of the friend hears `friends:asked` (`JoinAskView`), with the account, by the friend's name for it, the room's ID, which the friend's room page matches to show it, and how long it stands, and a connection that comes while it stands hears it too. The friend answers with an invitation (`friends:invite`), which the account then follows as any other, or ignores it (`friends:dismiss`); the account isn't told which. A request ends when it is answered so, when the account comes into the room, and after 10 minutes (`JOIN_ASK_MS`), when the friend leaves the room, when the room closes or when the two part. However it ends, at once or found by the sweep, every connection of the friend hears `friends:unasked`, and every connection of the account `friends:changed`, on which its pages offer to ask again; while it stands, the friend's card and the row say so (`FriendView.asked`). One request stands at a time to the same friend (`join_ask_pending`), and an account sends at most 10 an hour (`JOIN_ASKS_PER_HOUR`, `join_ask_limited`), counted only for those that go out. Nobody asks into a room they are in already (`already_in_room`), nor one that the chair removed them from (`removed`, `removed_recently`). Requests live in memory and are never kept.
- **Data.** The `friendships` table (migration 8): one row per pair, whichever side asked, with its status and when it was asked and answered, and (migration 16) the line of a request that is open and each side's name for the other. A friendship that is removed is deleted, its names with it, and a declined request is deleted once its wait is over; an account's rows go with it.

## Friend duos

What two friends have played together, and five titles for the friends who stand out (`shared/src/duos.ts`). Two accounts were teammates in a game when both have it on their records on the same side, and opponents when on different sides; every game on both records counts, whichever game it was and in a match or not, from before the two were friends too, but a game that an AI played in ([AI players](#ai-players)). Only friends count: a request counts for nothing, and the counts go when the friendship ends and come back when it is made again.

- **Counts.** For each friend: the games won and lost on the same side, and the games on different sides, with those the account won and those the friend won.
- **Titles.** Each is held by the friend with the most of one count, from three on, and friends level at the top share it: Co-First Author, the most games won together; Rejected Together, the most lost together; Your Reviewer 2, the most won against the account; Frequent Submitter, the most lost to it; Rival Reviewer, the most played against it. Each account has its own: Ann's Co-First Author may be Bob while Bob's is Cat.
- **Who sees them.** The account alone. Its friends list carries each friend's counts and titles (`FriendView.duo`) and sums up who holds which, and a friend's public profile carries what the two have played, for that friend alone (`ProfileView.duo`). When a game ends, the snapshot that ends it tells each account player of the titles that the game brought friends who played in it, who didn't hold them before (`RoomView.duos`); nobody else in the room is told, and the screens nothing.
- **Data.** Nothing new is kept. One query counts every friend's games from `game_records`, finding the others in each game by the index on the game (migration 13), whenever the friends list or a profile asks, and once for each account player with a friend in a game that ends. A deleted account's records go with it, and its counts with them.

## Public profiles

Every account has a public page, `/p/<handle>`, which anyone may open, with or without an account. The handle (`accounts.handle`, migration 11) is 12 hexadecimal digits drawn at random when the account is created, never changed and never reused, so that a link outlives a new username and nobody can count through the accounts by their numbers. Rooms, friend lists, requests and the list of likers carry it (`PlayerView.handle`, `FriendView.handle`, `LikerView.handle`), so that every way to a profile uses the same key.

- **The way to a profile:** a tap on an avatar opens a panel over the page, wherever the avatar is, so that nobody leaves a game or a page to look. In a room, the player's panel (`PlayerPanel.vue`): for an account, a brief card with the avatar, rank, number, day of registration and titles, and the whole page as a sheet of its own height over the panel, which it hides meanwhile, at most as tall as a sheet may be, past which it scrolls, with a round button back to it; for the viewer, their own card with the way to their account page, where they change it, and their whole page as others see it; for an AI player, its own page ([AI players](#ai-players)); for a guest, the avatar under the title that names them. Under the card, what the viewer may do with the player: what friends do, as the page itself offers it ([Friends](#friends)), the chair's actions, a report. Outside a room, on the account page and the home page, an account's panel (`ProfilePanel.vue`, one for the whole site, over whichever page opened it) is the same without what only a room has, and the friend search opens it too for the account it finds, as does an account among the feedback contributions on the home page, by its avatar or its username; elsewhere the names beside avatars are plain text. The page itself, `/p/<handle>`, is where a shared link leads, and nothing on the site leads there: the account page opens its owner's own in the panel. A page reached from another on the site has a round button back to it before its title, on a bar that stays at the top as the page scrolls while the masthead scrolls away ([design](design.md)), and one opened on its own, as from a shared link, has none: the wordmark leads home.
- **Shown to everyone:** username, avatar, rank, number, the day of registration, titles, the streak titles among them ([Streaks](#streaks)), the link and the line the owner wrote, and how many accounts like the page.
- **Shown if the owner chooses** (`show_record`, `show_thanks`; off until turned on): the record as counts, the same table as on the account page with the matches finished and won (`ProfileView.record`), and the feedback contributions as counts ([Feedback contributions](#feedback-contributions)). The points, the state of the email address, the friends and the games themselves never show.
- **The link** keeps the rules of a web link of a player (`checkWebLink` in `shared/src/feedback.ts`), which the lists of thanks take too: `https://` only, no user name, at most 200 characters. The page shows it as the address it is, opening in a new tab with `rel="nofollow ugc noopener"`. **The line** has at most 80 characters, cleaned as a contact is (`checkBio` in `shared/src/accounts.ts`), and is shown as text, never as markup. Both are optional; `POST /api/account/profile` sets them with the two choices.
- **Likes** (`likes`, migration 11). An account likes a page once and may take it back, never its own (`self_like`), 100 a day (`like_limited`). The count is public; who likes a page is shown to its owner alone (`GET /api/account/likers`), the latest 100 of them (`LIKERS_SHOWN`), with whether each is a friend, so that the owner may ask the others. The rows go with either account.
- **Requests waiting.** The owner's summary counts the friend requests waiting for an answer (`AccountSummary.requests`), for the badge on the home page and the account page; the page asks again on `friends:changed`.
- **The operator** clears a link and a line that break the rules with `admin.cjs profile clear <username> [--ban]`, which with `--ban` bars the account from writing them again (`profile_banned`; `profile allow` lifts it), and looks them over with `profile list` ([development.md](development.md#accounts-and-email)).

## Titles

An account keeps the titles that matches award ([Matches](#matches)) and those that games bring, such as Forgot to Submit for a PI who gives no clue in time and Deadline Hero for one who gives it in the last seconds ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#titles)), each as often as it was won, and its pages show each with the count (`TITLES` in `shared/src/accounts.ts`). A game names them in `PlayerResult.titles`, each with the turn it came in, and they go with the record, in its transaction: a game that goes on no record, withdrawn or played as a guest, brings none, nor does a game that an AI played in ([AI players](#ai-players)), and a turn of a game brings an account a title once (`titles_game`, migration 14). The table no longer fixes the titles with a CHECK: `TitleStore` refuses one that isn't in the code.

Every title that a page shows opens, at a tap, a sheet that says what it is and how it is won (`TitleInfo.vue`, `client/src/core/titles.ts`): the titles of matches and of games, the streaks, on the account page as the longest on the record and at the end of a game as the run so far, the feedback contributions, the author's badge, the decision of each game, on the record and in its letter, the mark of a game on the record that an AI played in, and the [friend titles](#friend-duos). The academic rank opens its ladder instead. Two places show a title that opens nothing: a screen, which nobody taps, shows them as text, and the rank beside a name in a room's list of players, where the avatar beside it opens the player's panel, with the rank and the titles on its card. What each says is in the locale files.

## Academic ranks

Shown only; they change nothing in the game. Every game that ends by itself is scored as a match scores it ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#matches)), in a match or not, and each account player's points go on their `game_records` row (migration 8). The snapshot that ends such a game carries the points of everyone in the room who played in it, guests too, with the game's detail of where they came from (`RoomView.points`), which the game shows as it ends ([games/keywords.md](games/keywords.md#points-as-they-come)); every snapshot of a game says whether an AI has played in it, so that it brings no points (`RoomView.agentsPlayed`). A withdrawn game counts for nothing, and a game is recorded once per account. The total holds a rank (`shared/src/ranks.ts`), thirteen of them: kindergartner from 0, primary school pupil from 30, middle school student from 80, high school student from 150, undergraduate from 300, master's student from 500, PhD student from 800, postdoc from 1200, lecturer from 1800, associate professor from 2500, professor from 4000, academician from 6000, and Reviewer 2 from 10000, the top, which the account page says has no next rank. The thresholds were set under the scoring before the PI's stakes, when a wrong card cost only the student who revealed it: a regular player, four evenings a week, made about 90 points a week, so primary school came in the first day or two, an undergraduate after about three weeks, a professor after about ten months and Reviewer 2 after about two years. Since every student pays for every wrong card and the PI pays more, a game gives less, and the ranks come slower; the thresholds stay as they were until play shows how much. A change of the table ranks every account by the new one at once. The rank travels with the player in every snapshot (`PlayerView.rank`), is read again when a game ends, when the account changes and at every sweep, and the account page shows the total and the way to the next rank. The rank on the account page and on a [public profile](#public-profiles) opens the ladder of every rank with its points, the player's own marked (`RankLadder.vue`); the public profile's ladder marks the rank alone, since the points never show there.

The rank is the sum of the points of every game on the account, whichever game they come from: a new game calibrates what a game gives to about what the first one gives, so that no game becomes the place to farm points.

## Streaks

How many games in a row an account has won, and lost, at most (`shared/src/streaks.ts`). They are counted over every game on the record, whichever game it was and in a match or not, in the order the games ended, counting only games with a result and without an AI ([AI players](#ai-players)): a withdrawn game has no row, watching is no game, and a guest has no record. The `streaks` table (migration 10) keeps one row per account, the longest run of wins, the longest run of losses, and the run the latest game is part of, written in the same transaction as the game's record (`RecordStore` in `server/src/store/records.ts`), so that the account page and the end of a game read one row however long the record is; migration 10 worked the rows out from the records that were there, and migration 22 again from those it left, so players have their history from the start.

A streak of three games or more is a title, named with the number in words (`shared/src/numbers.ts`), never a digit: Three Submissions, Three Acceptances, and Three Submissions, Three Rejections, with the number in Chinese characters in Chinese. The account page and the [public profile](#public-profiles) show the longest of each kind among the titles, the losing streak too, for anyone to see; only the notice at the end of a game is the player's alone. When a game ends, the snapshot that ends it carries each account player's streak of three or more (`RoomView.streaks`): a winning streak goes to everyone in the room, screens included, who cheer the player by name; a losing streak goes to its player alone, so that nobody is shown up. The player reads their own streak with a line for the occasion, drawn from a few per tier of length, and whether it is a new record for them. The snapshots of the waiting room and of the next game carry none.

### The account API

`ACCOUNT_API` in `shared/src/accounts.ts`. Answers are JSON, `{ ok: true, ... }` or `{ ok: false, error }`, with a matching status. HTTP because only an HTTP response can set a cookie that scripts never see.

| Address | Does |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/account` | The account, or null |
| `GET /api/account/records` | The record (login) |
| `POST /api/account/replay` | `gameId`; a game to watch again, for an account that played in it (login) |
| `POST /api/register` | `username`, `password`; logs in |
| `POST /api/login`, `/api/logout` | |
| `POST /api/account/username` | Renames (login) |
| `POST /api/account/avatar`, `.../avatar/remove` | The image itself, or the default (login) |
| `POST /api/account/password` | Every login ends; this browser gets a new one (login, password) |
| `POST /api/account/email`, `.../email/remove` | Mails a binding link, or unbinds (login, password) |
| `POST /api/email/confirm` | Binds (the link) |
| `POST /api/password/forgot` | Mails a reset link |
| `POST /api/password/reset` | Every browser logged out, this one in (the link) |

## Mail

Email is only for resetting a password. An address is bound through a mailed link, and the database keeps only its HMAC-SHA256 with a key derived from `SERVER_SECRET`. Without the secret and the mailer, the API answers `mail_unavailable`. A server started with another secret unbinds every address (`addresses_unbound`). Binding asks for the password; the link works once, for 24 hours. A forgotten password is answered the same for any address; a reset link works once, for 30 minutes, and logs out everywhere. Links carry the secret after `#`, so it never reaches a server or log; the database keeps its SHA-256 (`email_tokens`).

### The mailer

`mailer/` alone holds the mail service's key and writes two fixed mails in both languages (`mailer/src/locales/`). The game server asks over a Unix socket with one line of JSON of five fields, `{"v": 1, "kind", "to", "token", "language"}` (`shared/src/mail.ts`). It builds the link itself; at most 50 mails in 24 hours. It sends through Resend's API; `MAILER_TRANSPORT=print` writes each mail out as JSON instead, for development and tests.

## The agent

`agent/` asks the language model for the AI players' moves ([AI players](#ai-players)), so that the game server needs no network for it, as with the mailer. One line of JSON goes each way over a Unix socket (`AgentRequest` and `AgentReply` in `shared/src/agent.ts`, which each side checks with `AGENT_REQUEST` and `AGENT_REPLY`, refusing a field more or less): a `ping`, or a `clue` or a `guess` with the persona, how long the server waits (`waitMs`, at most 60 seconds, and 3 seconds short of the phase's end), the room's language and settings, the group, the board as the seat sees it and the clues so far. It asks the model through OpenAI's chat completions (`AGENT_MODEL_URL`, `AGENT_MODEL`, `AGENT_MODEL_KEY`), for JSON in a schema and with reasoning off:

- **A clue:** three candidates with the cards each is meant for, the best first, held against the room's rules with `checkClue` and against the board, the first clean one taken; twice more with the refused ones, and else `no_legal_clue`. In a Chinese room, a clue may be a single character, or a few that need not make a word, which often ties more cards together through the meanings of a character and the words it forms; where the room allows more than one character, at least one of the three is a single character.
- **Guesses:** the cards to reveal, the surest first, at least one and never more than the clue's number, with a sentence of why, which an AI student advising people writes to its group.

At most `AGENT_CONCURRENCY` requests go to the model at once and `AGENT_QUEUE` wait their turn; past them, `busy` at once, and a request whose turn hasn't come when its wait is over is `busy` then, while the server still listens. A model that answers nothing usable, or nothing in time, costs that request alone (`no_answer`); a service that can't be reached or refuses is `model_failed`, which takes the AI players offline. A ping lists the service's models, without loading one. With a key, the service's address is HTTPS, unless it is on the same machine, so that the key never goes over the network in the clear. The prompts are versioned (`PROMPT_VERSION`), and every answer notes the model and the version. The agent's log says what kind of request came, how long its answer took and whether it was one, never the board or the answer.

## Avatars

The page crops the picture and encodes it at 256 by 256 as WebP or JPEG, within 30 KB. **The server never decodes an image:** it reads the header only (type, size, still, at most 64 KB; `server/src/accounts/avatars.ts`). Files live in `avatars/` under `DATA_DIR`, named by SHA-256. Served under `/avatars/<name>` with `nosniff`, `default-src 'none'` and a year of `immutable` caching. The operator's `admin.cjs avatar remove <username> [--ban]` takes one down ([development.md](development.md#accounts-and-email)).

## Threat model

- **Guessed passwords:** slow salted scrypt and limits per address and name before hashing.
- **Who has an account:** answers that reveal a taken name are limited, `login_failed` is the same for a wrong name or password, and the friends messages answer a name that is no account's as they answer a stranger's, except the request, which costs one of the day's 30, and the search, which says whether a whole username is an account's, for one of the account's 30 an hour, counted before the name is looked up. It takes nothing but a whole name, so that nobody lists accounts by a part of a name or counts through them by number; a link to a page leads to the page, which its holder could open anyway.
- **Stolen logins and other sites:** a random secret, kept hashed, only in a hardened cookie, HTTPS only; `Origin` and `Sec-Fetch-Site` checks, no CORS, no framing; password change, binding and deletion ask for the password.
- **Links and mail:** 256 random bits after `#`, kept as hashes, short-lived, used once; two fixed mails from a mailer that alone holds the key.
- **Who plays with whom:** what two friends have played together, and the titles it earns, go to the two of them alone, each on their own friends list and on the other's profile; nobody else in a room or at a profile is sent them, and nothing of them is stored, since they are worked out from the records and the friendships when asked.
- **The agent and the model:** the agent is asked with no more than a seat may see, so an AI student never learns the key, and with no chat; its answers pass a strict schema and then the rules, as a player's messages do, so a model that answers nonsense, or a word of the chair's written to steer it, makes at most a bad clue, a wrong card, or a sentence of at most 200 characters in its own group's chat, where the AI student says why it chose a card.
- **A leaked database** holds usernames, password hashes, hashes of random secrets, keyed HMACs of email addresses, records with their points and titles, who is friends with whom and who asked whom, the lines of the requests not answered yet and the names that friends give each other, the links and the lines that accounts wrote on their profiles and who likes whose, the records of every game, with their boards, the clues and the groups, and their logs, with every move and every line of the groups' chats, when it came and the player ID that made it, and which player ID in a game was which account, which accounts may have AI players and the AI's logs, with the models' clues and reasons, the feedback, with the contacts of the last 90 days and the user agents of bug reports, and the reports, with who reported whom, the name the player had, in which room and the note; no IP addresses.

## Logging

One JSON object per line on stdout, never a token: rooms created and closed (`empty`, `idle`, `stale`, `chair`, `restart`), games started and ended, errors, the `stats` line, and named events such as `listening`, `startup_failed`, `database_opened`, `snapshot_failed`, `storage_full`, `feedback_received`, `feedback_purged`, `report_received`, `account_created`, `mail_not_sent`, `words_clashed`, `match_ended`, `game_log_too_large`, `agent_added`, `agent_failed`, `agent_move_refused`, `agent_down`, `agent_up`, `thanks_list_read` and `thanks_list_skipped`; Node's warnings come as `warning` lines, so every line is JSON.

## Status

`server/src/stats.ts` counts for both the log and the page: no addresses, tokens, names or room codes. The `stats` line, every 5 minutes: rooms, members, connections by transport and country, memory, and on a server with an agent, the AI players in rooms, whether the agent answers and their steps of the last hour, which the status page shows too. The status page, with `STATUS_PORT`, listens on `127.0.0.1` only, which is why it needs no password, and answers only a `Host` of `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `[::1]`, with any port or none, as a browser on the same machine sends it, whatever local port it reaches the page through; anything else gets a bare 421, so that a page elsewhere can't read it, contacts and all, by pointing a name of its own at the address (DNS rebinding). `/` is the page, `/status.json` the numbers, `/feedback` what players sent, the open feedback, all of it or that of one status (`?status=`), each with its status and trail, who it is from: the account that signed it, by its username as it is now, or that the account is gone, a guest's name and whether it is an account's username, or no name with the account it was sent from, and once adopted the two sentences of its contribution, and in the trash with the days it has left; `/reports` their reports of each other ([Public rooms](#public-rooms)); of accounts, the page shows only how many there are and how many are new in the last 7 days. The pages are read-only: GET alone, no script, no form, nothing loaded from elsewhere, `Cache-Control: no-store`, English only; what became of the feedback is changed with `admin.cjs` ([Feedback](#feedback)), whose commands for each piece the feedback page shows as text to copy, with `ADMIN_COMMAND` in front, and never runs; where the operator has a command of their own that takes the same subcommands, and `publish <id>`, which publishes a piece of feedback as an issue in one go (`FEEDBACK_COMMAND`), the page shows every command with it in front instead, and `publish` first for feedback that has no issue yet, before the commands to do it by hand, and `resolve`, which adopts the feedback as it records the pull request, in place of `adopt`. Countries come from Cloudflare's `CF-IPCountry` header, read only with `TRUST_PROXY` on; any other proxy must drop it ([development.md](development.md#running-in-production)).
